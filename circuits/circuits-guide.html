Executive overview

A DataChip is a reusable computational capability with a stated purpose and boundaries. A Circuit connects compatible capabilities to complete a larger task. A Circuit Genome records which components, versions and approved relationships belong to that assembly. This handbook concerns use, accountability and qualification—not program source or implementation APIs.

01 — Components, circuits and genomes

A DataChip may perform a calculation, classification, measurement, reconstruction or bounded inference. Each one needs an identity, supported input class, expected output, limitations and an evidence record.

A Circuit is the working arrangement of multiple chips. A Genome preserves the versioned composition and the rules required to identify or restore it. A diagram is a way to understand the system; it is not by itself proof of correct execution.

02 — Where components come from

A qualified capability can originate in conventional code, an approved scientific process, an existing BTG subsystem or a candidate proposed by NIKI. Provenance and license constraints travel with its record.

A model-generated suggestion, copied fragment or visually assembled card must not be treated as a novel, owned invention until authorship, rights, originality and test evidence have been established.

03 — What a trustworthy chip record contains

A reader should expect a plain-language purpose, version, approved use, unit or data assumptions, accepted and rejected input categories, required dependencies, error behavior, quality evidence and owner or custodian.

Evaluation must specify whether a result is known correct, statistically supported, limited to a bounded test range or not verified. High test counts are meaningful only within the stated dataset and oracle.

04 — Connecting chips safely

Two components may be individually valid yet incompatible because their units, input shapes, timing, data permissions or error handling differ. The combined Circuit must therefore be tested as a system.

Responsible assembly considers termination, resource use, recoverability, incorrect output propagation and whether a step could access material outside its permitted mandate.

05 — Circuit Genomes and versions

A Genome is a traceable recipe for a particular configuration of components and their connections. It supports evaluation of what changed, what remained qualified and whether a previously tested configuration can be reconstructed.

Replacing one chip, dependency or data source can change system behavior. An older qualification should not be silently carried forward to a materially different Genome.

06 — The Composer experience

BTG’s Composer is intended to present conventional source editing and visual composition together. Users may inspect a graph, understand dependencies and assemble permitted components under controlled conditions.

A drag-and-drop composition is not the same as an automatically verified program. The operator must see unconnected inputs, missing components, unsupported operations and unresolved rights before approval.

07 — Human authority and automated invention

NIKI may propose a new chip or repair. It should preserve candidate evidence and failures, then submit outputs to independent tests. ADAM and ENTITY govern whether a result can be promoted or used for a protected purpose.

Automatically generated algorithms do not receive ownership, safety certification or unrestricted deployment authority merely because they compile or pass internal tests.

08 — Operations and incident response

Operational records should identify a Circuit version, operator mandate, material input category, run outcome, resource limits and relevant error or integrity evidence. Users should be able to isolate a failed chip rather than losing the provenance of an entire run.

Where private source material or personal data is involved, incident reports must expose only what authorized reviewers need; public documentation should contain no implementation secrets.

09 — Commercial rights and valuation

A reproducible Circuit could have value as a licensed capability, transferred intellectual property or part of a permitted investment structure. Those are not identical transactions.

Commercial admission requires verifiable rights, useful performance, reproducibility, contract restrictions and truthful disclosure of unproven features. A visual graph alone is not a market asset.

10 — Qualification and current limitations

BTG publicly identifies Composer and runtime engineering demonstrations with bounded source-parity and test scopes. Those records do not establish universal conversion of arbitrary programs or unrestricted production safety.

Before operational use, the specific version must have its own release, compatibility and security evidence. The complete proprietary implementation is not published in this library.
