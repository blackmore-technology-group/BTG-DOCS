Executive overview

NIKI is BTG’s autonomous research and invention programme. Its ambition is not merely to answer questions but to propose computational methods, test them against defined evidence, learn from failed candidates, repair them where possible and retain only demonstrated capabilities. It remains subject to human authority and independent qualification.

01 — NIKI’s intended role

Given an authorized problem, NIKI may study allowed knowledge, generate candidate algorithms or Circuits and seek an executable answer. It is intended to work with FarmNodes, DataChips, BTDU, ENTITY and ADAM rather than replace them.

An articulate explanation or passing compilation is not sufficient. The important distinction is whether the new method performs against independent test cases and reproduces its result in a controlled environment.

02 — The research mandate

A responsible sponsor describes the actual problem, permitted data, desired output, independent acceptance criteria, computational budget, safety rules and qualified evaluator.

Without a clear oracle or measure of success, NIKI may produce interesting material that cannot be defended as a verified invention. The mandate must also prohibit unauthorized access, dangerous actions and leakage of protected information.

03 — Candidate generation and preservation

The system should retain the original candidate, its input assumptions, resource conditions and test results. Any revision becomes a separately identifiable candidate so a later success does not erase an earlier failure.

A suggested repair that leaves the source unchanged or cannot execute due to missing dependencies is not a successful repair. A promising design remains a hypothesis until tested.

04 — Independent tests and adversarial evaluation

A strong evaluation uses tests not simply memorized or supplied as worked examples to the inventing process. It should include boundary values, failure cases, alternative runtimes and resource restrictions appropriate to the task.

A perfect result over a bounded local oracle does not prove all possible inputs or transfer to a new problem class. Reviewers need to see test size, candidate identity, failed runs and whether test data were held out.

05 — Learning from errors

A useful research cycle identifies where a proposal deviated from the expected result, forms an explanation and produces a meaningfully altered candidate or testable revision. Retaining unsuccessful approaches helps avoid repeating the same nonrepair.

The goal is evidence-based improvement, not unrestricted self-modification. Proposed changes must pass controlled admission gates before they affect production applications.

06 — DataChips and Circuit Genomes

A validated NIKI method can be packaged as a versioned reusable capability with a clear input/output contract and associated evidence. A Circuit Genome can preserve the relationships among several such capabilities.

Promotion depends on compatibility, ownership, licence rights, and operator approval. A local success must not automatically provide access to unrelated devices, financial systems or personal data.

07 — FarmNode collaboration

A research task may be worked on by one logical farm and challenged by another. Independent host or institutional review can test whether a candidate works outside its originating environment.

Multiple processes on the same desktop do not establish independent scientific replication. A valid external challenge must document what the reviewer actually reran or independently re-created.

08 — The role of language models

A language model such as a Qwen-family system may help with reasoning, coding and idea generation, if its contribution is measured against the existing NIKI algorithm-invention baseline. It is a tool within the architecture, not a replacement for NIKI’s experiment, validation and ownership framework.

Model size does not demonstrate practical improvement. Hardware cost, reproducibility, measured algorithm quality and safety must govern whether a model is introduced.

09 — Failures and bounded evidence

BTG’s public historical records include a sensor repair later passing all 65,536 cases of one local oracle and a distinct Granby candidate that produced 256 incorrect cases out of 65,536 in its recorded test. The two results address different tasks and cannot be conflated.

The existence of generated repair source is not the same as a qualified changed algorithm. Independent runtime and dependency qualification must accompany future claims of success.

10 — Safety, intellectual property and release

New invention records should document provenance, permitted source materials, potential third-party rights, dangerous or prohibited uses, disclosure restrictions and lawful commercial classification.

NIKI should not grant itself rights, issue investment securities or decide that a technically functioning output is legally marketable. Responsible owners and independent reviewers must approve those actions.

11 — Current status and the next evidence gate

The intended end-state includes reproducible invention, robust self-repair, independently qualified execution environments and release-ready reusable components. Public material shows meaningful bounded progress but not unrestricted general invention or fully qualified physical multi-node production.
