Executive overview

Bit Trade Global is the proposed stock-market-style exchange for qualifying datasets, algorithms and inventions. Its public information model separates the underlying asset, the legal issuer, the rights being offered, the instrument identifier, and the market record. Commercial licensing and investment securities are different activities governed by different laws.

01 — What the Exchange is

The Exchange gives a defined data or invention asset its own public information record so that rights, utility, evidence, restrictions, and potentially investment interest can be evaluated independently of the company using it. The asset can be commercially valuable without being a security, and an issuer can exist without that issuer having completed a lawful offering.

A ticker is an identification and navigation device, not proof that an instrument has been admitted to a regulated market. The Exchange must distinguish a proposed listing, an approved commercial product and a legally issued transferable investment.

02 — Three classes of economic instrument

U-class commercial-use rights permit specified access to or use of a dataset, algorithm, service or Circuit under a licence. The purchaser receives the agreed utility, not underlying source ownership or corporate equity.

A-class instruments concern genuinely transferable data-related or intellectual-property rights under a valid assignment or eligible rights contract. I-class instruments represent investments or other securities created and authorized by a legal issuer. A revenue promise, note, share, fund interest or profit-based instrument may trigger securities rules regardless of its marketing label.

03 — The asset, issuer and instrument records

The asset record explains exactly what the data or invention is, its version, documented origin, limitations, evidence and permitted disclosure. A rights record identifies the party claiming authority to grant rights and any restrictions or third-party interests.

An issuer record separately identifies the legal entity, persons accountable, filings, governance and material financial information that is actually verified. An instrument sheet defines the holder’s entitlement, transferability, maturity or duration, fees, risks, required legal documents and whether it is issued, proposed or inactive.

04 — From candidate to potential listing

The intended path begins with a proposed data asset and source evidence. Rights, privacy and technical qualifications are checked before a candidate can be classified. A commercial use right may proceed by suitable contract; an investment must pass issuer, offering, venue and participant eligibility requirements.

A candidate must not be presented as live because a symbol, simulated order book or record has been created. A change in underlying dataset, source licences, issuer arrangements or instrument terms can require renewed review.

05 — How prices are formed

A valuation is an estimate supported by a stated method and inputs. A seller’s asking price is not a completed transaction. A quoted bid is what a buyer offers, and an ask is what a seller requests. When a lawful market accepts matching orders, a transaction price may be recorded.

Asset-appraisal value, royalty or licence value, issuer enterprise value and a security market price are four different figures. The Exchange must display the correct price type, currency, timestamp, available quantity and whether it is simulated, indicative or executed.

06 — Orders, holdings and transaction evidence

The software design includes account-held instruments, bid-and-ask orders, order cancellations, transaction history and ledger entries. An executed trade should have a unique identifier, authorizations, timestamp, rights effects and immutable or tamper-evident evidence.

A ledger record does not itself move bank money or establish ownership in the external world. Legal title, lawful settlement, fiat reconciliation, custody and entitlements require the responsible institutions and valid agreements.

07 — Fees, financial arrangements and disclosure

BTG’s working concept makes registration, listing and ticker issuance free. A proposed transaction-revenue schedule has included a percentage plus a fixed component; a binding fee schedule must be published and approved before use, specifying currency, taxes, refunds, and what constitutes a completed transaction.

Investment proceeds and client funds must never be treated as company income merely because an exchange interface displays them. Disclosure must cover asset risks, issuer financial position where applicable, conflicting interests, transfer restrictions and fees.

08 — Investment in data value

A properly constituted asset-focused company could hold the necessary rights to exploit a particular dataset or invention and, subject to authorization, issue securities that give investors legally defined exposure to its economic performance. The shareholder owns a security of the issuer, not automatic title to raw data.

This is the intended distinction from a one-time data licence. Potential future profit depends on real customers, qualified technology, lawful rights, commercial execution, the issuer’s costs, market conditions and the security’s legally documented terms. No value or return is assured.

09 — Risk, privacy and legal boundaries

Market records must avoid publishing sensitive source data, credentials, confidential methods or protected personal information. A commercial buyer does not automatically gain every upstream record used during discovery, and an investor does not acquire broader access than the stated security provides.

Operational, model, cybersecurity, pricing, liquidity, conflict-of-interest and counterparty risks can materially affect an instrument. The public portal is informational, not a solicitation to invest or evidence of securities-exchange recognition.

10 — Evidence, status and public accountability

The current public site describes engineering demonstrations, sandbox market workflows, candidate instrument profiles and an evidence register. Live securities issuance, custody, payment rails and recognized exchange operation have not been independently demonstrated as production-approved.

The public library must show dated evidence separately from product intentions. Each claim should identify its exact test scope and should not turn local software passes into an external certification.
