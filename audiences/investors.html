The three instruments are economically different

ClassWhat is purchasedWhat is NOT automatically purchased
U — commercial useDefined contractual permission to use data, an algorithm or delivered service.Shares, raw data title or profit participation.
A — transferable rightsSpecified assignment, transferable IP rights or other expressly documented economic entitlements.A security exemption or proof of ownership by hash.
I — investment securitiesAn actual security issued by a qualified legal issuer, with rights set by its terms.A piece of raw data or guaranteed return.

How a single asset might support an issuer

Imagine a company whose principal business is licensing one robotics recovery dataset. Subject to incorporation, lawful rights, professional advice and securities requirements, that company could issue a class of shares. An investor’s claim would be on that issuer under its legal documents, not direct possession of the source dataset. An illustrative C$1 share price would not be a verified valuation or current offer. No proposed terms in these public pages should be taken as issued instruments.

Follow the money, not just the ticker

For any securities subscription, verify where investor funds actually go, the entity receiving them, escrow or custodian terms, whether securities are legally issued and what official register proves ownership. For commercial licensing, verify price, contract, delivery, invoice and bank reconciliation. These are separate ledgers and legal acts.

Understand the downside

  • A dataset might be useful but never produce profitable customers.
  • Asset value and share value are not interchangeable. Investors can lose all capital.
  • A platform may have no lawful resale venue; liquidity can be zero.
  • Rights can expire or be challenged; private information may be unusable even if technically available.
  • Issuers may create additional shares or incur expenses and debts that dilute returns.
  • Conflicts can arise among BTG, an issuer, source contributors, brokers and investors.

What the documentation cannot replace

Not a prospectus, offering memorandum, independent appraisal, current financial statement or legal eligibility decision. A sophisticated investor should require the specific issuer’s signed original offering documents, named professionals, actual audit scopes, contract rights and regulator or registration information before taking any action. No deposit instructions or investor enrolment are provided in this public library.
