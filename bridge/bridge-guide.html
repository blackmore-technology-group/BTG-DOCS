Executive overview

BTG Bridge is the participant-controlled data connection and permission layer. It can connect approved information sources to governed work, but a connected source is not automatically a tradable asset, ownership transfer or investment. The purpose is to make participation and authorized use understandable and accountable.

01 — Why Bridge exists

A person or organization may hold useful information across devices, software, repositories or records. Bridge is designed to allow specific, consented participation without making every underlying record available to the Exchange or to an unknown buyer.

Bridge’s job is permissioned intake and evidence of approved use. ENTITY addresses authority and rights, NIKI and FarmNodes perform authorized research, BTDU retains qualified knowledge, and the Exchange deals with distinct, lawful rights-defined instruments.

02 — The four separate decisions

Connection establishes an authenticated relationship to a source. Permission authorizes a named purpose and scope. Transfer or computation permits information to move or be processed only under those conditions. Commercialization requires additional enforceable rights.

Treating those four decisions as one checkbox would undermine the design. The interface must give the source owner a comprehensible explanation of what is being sought, by whom, for how long and for what benefit or risk.

03 — Permission details to inspect

The participant should be able to examine source category, selected fields, purpose, recipient, geographic controls, retention period, permitted processing, onward sharing, expiry and withdrawal rules. Consent should be recorded as a scoped decision, not inferred from merely installing an app.

Special care is needed for shared workplace data, minors, clinical information, third-party identities and material subject to confidentiality agreements. Possessing a copy of information is not proof of authority to authorize its resale.

04 — From raw source to permitted result

A permitted research operation may transform, aggregate or analyze approved inputs and return a limited result. The raw information can remain with a source custodian when the authorized process permits compute-to-data rather than copying entire datasets.

A useful discovery could become the subject of a separate asset-qualification record. It still requires checks for privacy leakage, scientific validity, source restrictions, ownership, derivative rights and valid contracts before any commercial or investment proposal.

05 — Source categories and compatibility

Potential sources include individual devices, approved applications, organization-controlled systems and permitted file or research collections. Each connector requires its own authentication, field mapping, legal permission and testing.

Not every named provider, handset or account is currently connected. An illustrated source card or demonstration does not establish that a production provider has granted access, that refresh tokens are recoverable or that deletion is supported.

06 — Revocation and retention

A participant may withdraw future access within the rules of the applicable agreement and law. A usable revocation flow needs a clear effective date, acknowledgement and what it will stop.

Withdrawal cannot literally undo a permitted transfer that has already occurred, erase a recipient’s legally required records or revoke a separately lawful entitlement. Where deletion is requested, the outcome must distinguish completed deletion, pending propagation and legitimate retention exceptions.

07 — Security and identity

Bridge is intended to use authenticated source relationships, precise scope checks, encrypted records, verifiable decision history and separation of participant accounts. These safeguards require production testing against cross-account leakage, key recovery and real provider behavior.

Never enter credentials into an unfamiliar page, approve a broader scope than necessary or share raw access tokens with any market participant. Access to an app is not evidence that a private key or underlying record is safe.

08 — Rights and payment boundaries

Contributing data does not automatically mean selling it. A separate agreement must address rights to process inputs, ownership of any derivative work, possible contributor benefit and how disputes would be resolved.

Bridge is not a bank, an exchange wallet or a guarantee that activity will produce monetary returns. A participant may opt into permitted research without any investment or data-product listing occurring.

09 — Institutional adoption

An organization should examine lawful basis, roles of controller and processor, data residency, incident handling, consent evidence, record minimization, retention, connector validation and audit access.

Third-party security claims should be identified by auditor, dates, scope and exclusions. Local encryption tests are useful engineering evidence but do not independently certify an entire consumer service.

10 — Development status and safe expectations

Public BTG evidence describes local permission and encrypted-core demonstrations. Real multi-user consumer identity, independently assessed isolation, provider-approved connector deployment and operational recovery must still be evaluated.

The manual describes the intended accountable participant experience, not a promise that all screens, connectors and controls are available for production use today.
